## Brand Voice Characteristics

### 1. Bold and declarative
ahead does not hedge. Sentences are short and sure of themselves. No "we try to" or "we aim to" — just statements of fact delivered as if the outcome is already inevitable.

Examples: "Wir treten nicht gegen die Klassiker an." / "WE DON'T COMPETE. WE REPLACE."

### 2. English-German bilingual mix
Headlines and chapter titles are frequently in English (all-caps), while body copy is in German (lowercase, informal). This creates a visual rhythm — the big idea lands in English, the explanation comes in German. It signals: European by culture, global in ambition.

Examples: "UNSERE GESCHICHTE" mixed with "THE WORLD'S TASTIEST REVOLUTION"

### 3. Informal and direct ("du" address)
Always addresses the customer as "du" (informal you). Never "Sie". The tone is that of a slightly older, cooler friend who happens to know a lot about nutrition.

Examples: "Du hast 60 Tage, um dich zu verlieben." / "Probier selbst!"

### 4. Playfully irreverent
ahead uses wit and lightness to defuse the "health food" stigma. They're fully aware that "better for you" brands are often joyless — and they position themselves as the antidote.

Examples:
- "Kein Deal, kein Problem" (after being rejected on Höhle der Löwen)
- "Tasting tiiiiime!"
- "Scale-Up Mode aktiviert"
- "Null Plan B"
- "Wild!"

### 5. Emotionally honest and self-aware
The brand story doesn't shy away from failure (the VC rejection, the early struggles). This makes ahead feel authentic — a brand built by real people, not a marketing machine.

Timeline moments that show this: "Höhle der Löwen: Kein Deal, kein Problem — Pitch im April, Sendung im Oktober. Die Löwen winken ab. Tausende neue Fans winken uns zu."

### 6. Conviction, not compliance
ahead doesn't say "we reduced sugar because guidelines recommend it" — they say sugar is unnecessary. The framing is ideological, not regulatory.

Example: **"Zucker: Braucht kein Mensch"** — sugar is something nobody needs, not something you're reluctantly reducing.

## Unique Snacking Points (USPs in brand language)

These are the exact six pillars ahead uses in their "About" section:

1. **Zucker: Braucht kein Mensch**
   "Wir verzichten auf zugesetzten Zucker. Und du? Auf nichts."
   Max. 50g sugar/day (per DGE guidelines) — ahead helps you stay under without sacrificing variety.

2. **Geschmack, der für sich spricht**
   "Viele Low-Sugar-Alternativen schmecken nach Alternative. Unsere Snacks schmecken nach Snacks: cremig, knusprig, sündhaft gut – so, wie du sie dir wünschst."

3. **Kalorien-ärmer als die Klassiker**
   "Nimm deine Lieblings-Snacks von früher. Dann nimm unsere. Weniger Kalorien = noch mehr Spaß."

4. **Protein snacken geht einfacher**
   "Genug Protein am Tag? Gar nicht so leicht. Unsere Snacks können deinen täglichen Bedarf unterstützen."

5. **Mit wertvollen Ballaststoffen**
   "Wir helfen gerne nach. Zum Beispiel wenn es darum geht, auf die empfohlenen 30 g Ballaststoffe zu kommen."

6. **Schmeckt's nicht? Geld zurück**
   "Du hast 60 Tage, um dich zu verlieben. Klappt nicht? Kein Problem: wir erstatten dir alles."

## Language to Avoid / What ahead is NOT

- Clinical / pharmaceutical (no white coat tone)
- Deprivation-focused ("eat less", "restrict yourself")
- Preachy about health
- Apologetic about taste
- Mainstream-corporate
- Fear-based ("sugar kills you")

ahead's enemies: blandness, compromise, and the idea that healthy food has to be a sacrifice.
